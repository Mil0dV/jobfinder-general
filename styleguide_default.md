# Cover Letter Style Guide (built-in default)

This is the neutral fallback voice used when no personal `Cover-letter styleguide.md`
exists. For letters that actually sound like you, run `cli.py --setup` with a couple of
your own cover letters, or copy `Cover-letter styleguide.md.example` and fill it in.

---

## Overall Tone & Intent

* **Direct, personal, informal-but-sharp.**
* Avoid generic polish or corporate-speak — write like someone who knows what they're doing and says it plainly.
* Sound like a real person motivated by purpose and the work itself, not status or platitudes.
* Let authentic curiosity, alignment, and track record speak louder than ego.

---

## Structure & Flow

* **Opening:** A specific hook — how/where the job came to attention, or something about the company/product that resonates.
* **Middle:** Connect real experience to what the role needs — pragmatically, with just enough detail to be credible.
* **Close:** Clean and confident, e.g. *"I look forward to expanding on this in an interview."* Avoid gushing sign-offs.
* **Total length:** ~150–220 words. Digestible, no fluff. Ideally fits on one screen.

---

## Content Priorities

* Lead with **relevance** — map what the candidate has done to what the company needs.
* Use real, tangible examples from the resume: projects shipped, tools used, problems solved. Never invent.
* Show **cultural fit** — that the candidate gets how they work and why.
* Inject **authentic motivation** — a line or two on why this work/company matters, not fake passion.

---

## Grammar, Style, and Quirks

* **Use sentence fragments intentionally, but sparingly** — good for rhythm; don't stack them.
* **Avoid em-dashes (—).** Prefer commas, colons, semicolons, or a plain hyphen (`-`).
* **Favor short, readable paragraphs.** Break at natural pivots.
* **Minimalist punctuation.** Don't overuse ellipses or parentheses.
* **Use contractions sparingly.** Mixing formal ("I have") and casual ("I'd") adds texture.
* **Use precise verbs.** No "leveraging synergies" or "driving impact." Build, debug, migrate, improve, fix.
* **Avoid résumé-isms** like "fast-paced environment," "team player," or "stakeholder alignment."
* **Avoid the dramatic backwards setup** — describing something then pivoting with "— that's exactly the kind of X I want to be building." It reads as performed enthusiasm. Just say it plainly.
