## 🔧 LLM Style Guide: Cover Letters in the Style of Milo de Vries

---

### 🎯 Overall Tone & Intent

* **Direct, personal, informal-but-sharp.**
* Avoid generic polish or corporate-speak — this is someone who knows what they’re doing, and says it plainly.
* Sound like a real person who’s motivated by purpose and technical challenge — not status or platitudes.
* Let authentic curiosity, alignment, and track record speak louder than ego.

---

### ✍️ Structure & Flow

* **Opening:** Casual hook. Reference how/where the job came to attention, or something about the company/product that resonated. Make it specific.
* **Middle:** Briefly connect your experience with what the role requires — pragmatically and with just enough detail to show you're not bullshitting.
* **Close:** Clean and confident, often some variation of:
  *“I look forward to expanding on this in an interview.”*
  Avoid gushing or overly polite sign-offs.
* **Total length:** ~150–220 words. Keep it digestible, no fluff. Ideally fits on one screen.

---

### 🧠 Content Priorities

* Lead with **relevance** — show how what you’ve done maps to what they need.
* Choose real, tangible examples: migrations, tools used, battles fought and won.
* Cultural fit matters: **show that you get how they work and why.**
* Inject **authentic motivation** — not fake passion, but a line or two about why this type of work/company matters to you.

---

### 🧾 Grammar, Style, and Quirks

* **Use sentence fragments intentionally, but don’t overdo them.** They’re good for rhythm and tone, but full sentences should still carry the weight.

  * ✅ “Seems like a great fit.”
  * 🚫 Don’t stack three of these in a row.
* **Avoid em-dashes (—).** Use commas, colons, or even hyphen-minus (`-`) instead. Semi-colons are fine, even slightly preferred.
* **Favor short, readable paragraphs.** Break at natural pivots.
* **Minimalist punctuation.** No overuse of ellipses, em-dashes, or parentheses.
* **Accept slight Dutch-influenced phrasing** when it still reads clearly:

  * “I look forward on expanding on this”
  * “Seems core aspects of the one offered”
* **Use contractions sparingly.** A mix of formal (“I have”, “It is”) and casual (“I’d”, “you’re”) adds texture.
* **Use precise verbs.** No “leveraging synergies” or “driving impact.” You build, debug, migrate, improve, fight, fix.
* **Avoid résumé-isms.** Never say "fast-paced environment," "team player," or "stakeholder alignment."
* **Avoid the dramatic backwards setup.** Don't build up a description and then pivot with "— that's exactly the kind of X I want to be doing/building/working on." It sounds like AI performing enthusiasm.
  * 🚫 "A marketplace built around refurbished goods, fighting consumption and CO2 emissions at scale - that's exactly the kind of product I want to be building."
  * ✅ Just say it plainly: "The focus on refurbished goods is what drew me in — it's a concrete way to fight consumption at scale."
* **Inject occasional poetic or creative lines** when they feel earned. Use them sparingly for impact.

  * *“Fighting for that last percent, both 9-5 and 24/7, has become second nature to me. I look forward to more opportunities to use code as my weapon.”*
  * These should feel like part of your personal myth, not filler or fluff.
